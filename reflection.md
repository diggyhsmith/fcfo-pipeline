**What was hardest to build.** The verifier was harder than the extraction. IBISWorld pages are two-column, so ordinary text extraction interleaves the columns and a quote that wraps across lines is no longer contiguous; I had to build a column-aware version of each page. IBISWorld tables also hide private-use icon glyphs between a label and its number, which broke an exact match on the revenue line of the At a Glance page. Worst, my first fuzzy-matching rule (accept at 90 or above) let a quote through after I changed "grew" to "shrank", a score of 95. I tightened it so every word and digit must survive. Then all 79 real quotes passed anyway, so the verifier caught nothing in the extraction itself. Its value is that it could have, and the self-test shows it rejects corrupted quotes.

**A decision the tool cannot support.** It cannot tell us how to price a monthly retainer for a $5M-revenue client. That needs what fractional CFOs charge, hours per client and what owners will pay, and none of the seven signals or three reports contain a price. The closest numbers are whole-industry profit margins (11.0% for management consulting, 16.4% for accounting) and a consumer tax-return fee of $323 from a 2021 survey. It also cannot say whether the $2M-$20M band buys this service, because IBISWorld splits customers by type, not by size. The missing input is niche-level unit economics, which only interviews or public rate cards would supply.

**What running it showed that the framework did not.** Reading Five Forces suggests each force has one rating. Running it showed the rating depends on which proxy industry you choose. Regulation is Low in management consulting (54161) but High and increasing in accounting (54121c); buyer power is High in one and Moderate in the other. My reconciliation rule picks 54161 because it sits nearest our NAICS code, yet founders who practice as CPAs would operate under the accounting rules, so the "low regulation" headline could mislead. Market share told a similar story: PwC holds 9.7% of accounting but a 2.5-5% band of consulting, and no report gives any share for the niche. The 54121c report even contradicts itself ($158.4bn against $157.4bn), and First Research's industry-code page came out empty, so its scope had to be inferred from the title. The framework never warned me that the hardest part is deciding what the industry is.
